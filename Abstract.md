The Sugarscape model of Epstein and Axtell is a canonical agent-based testbed for the emergence of economic and social phenomena from simple local rules, but its agents are fundamentally prescribed: movement and trade follow hand-coded heuristics designed to produce specific macro-level outcomes. This project asks instead what strategies arise when one trader is replaced by a learning agent with no hand-coded rules, embedded in a population of classical rule-based traders.
We implement the two-commodity Sugarscape G1mt variant in MESA, wrap a single agent in a Gymnasium-compatible interface, and train it with Proximal Policy Optimisation under both a fixed-profile regime and a profile-conditioned masked regime using MaskablePPO. Across four reward functions and five evaluation scenarios, the trained policy matches or exceeds the rule-based population on survival, wealth, and trade volume, while organically reproducing classical Sugarscape phenomena such as bilateral exchange and price stability. The reward ablation reveals a clear behavioural gradient from stationary subsistence to mobile trade-seeking, the masked policy generalises substantially better to scarcity and spatial alterations, and a flat survival reward under an asymmetric metabolic profile produces a striking instance of reward hacking, in which the agent discovers a homeostatic two-cell oscillation and withdraws entirely from the economy. 

The project contributes a reusable single-agent RL wrapper around a full MESA Sugarscape population, a diagnostic visualisation suite, and an empirical characterisation of how perceptual limits, metabolic demands, and reward design jointly shape learned economic behaviour.
